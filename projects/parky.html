INDEPENDENT PRODUCT BUILDER · ANDROID

Parky — helping drivers remember where they parked, offline

Built a focused parking-memory experience that combines GPS with the contextual clues a map pin alone may miss.

Public evidence repository ↗

The user need

Parking retrieval is not just a navigation issue. Drivers may know the general lot but forget a garage level, pillar or bay; GPS alone may not resolve the final few metres. The hypothesis: saving position plus a small amount of human context makes returning to a vehicle easier and less stressful.

What I built and owned

I independently defined the MVP, documented flows and business rules, implemented it in React Native / TypeScript, and validated an Android build on a physical device. The app explicitly saves a parking location using up to six seconds of GPS sampling, communicates reported accuracy, accepts optional notes and private photos, and provides offline distance and directional guidance. Local history, a widget, Quick Settings and launcher shortcuts reduce repeat-use friction.

Key product decisions & tradeoffs

  • Explicit save over auto-detection: more predictable, with no background location or vehicle pairing—at the cost of relying on users to save.
  • Show GPS uncertainty: honesty about weak positioning encourages useful contextual clues instead of false precision.
  • Offline compass over embedded maps: no API keys or map-server dependency, but cannot navigate around walls, roads or garage floors.
  • Local-only storage: avoids account setup and cloud-location history, but offers no automatic restoration across devices.

Evidence, limits and next validation

A standalone APK was built and installed on a Samsung Android device. The supporting repository documents platform integrations, TypeScript checks and release-readiness gaps. Formal customer outcomes and successful vehicle-recovery rates have not been measured. The next experiment is a small observed save-and-find test across open-air and multilevel parking environments, including weak-signal and permission-denial cases.
