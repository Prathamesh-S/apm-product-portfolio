INDEPENDENT ANALYTICAL CASE

Peak-Hour Delivery Recovery

A hypothetical analysis of late-order cancellations, targeted compensation, and experiment economics.

Read detailed PDF ↗

The problem

Demand spikes and courier constraints can delay orders; blanket incentives may increase cost without solving the underlying supply issue.

What I owned

Created a structured diagnosis, experiment framework, and financial comparison for a food-delivery cancellation problem.

Solution and scope

Separated root-cause investigation from communication interventions, considered segment targeting, and evaluated a notification-versus-coupon experiment.

Three-dimensional product thinking: tradeoffs

Business contribution over headline cancellation reduction; severity and incremental impact over blanket coupons; test before scaling.

Evidence and outcome

Under illustrative assumptions, reducing cancellations from 14% to 11% with an average ₹18 incentive cost per eligible order implies ₹600 spent per additional cancellation avoided before lifetime-value considerations.

Evidence boundary

All experiment numbers are illustrative scenario inputs—not observed Swiggy, Zomato, or real-user results.
