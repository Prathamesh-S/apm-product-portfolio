INDEPENDENT FEATURE DESIGN · CONCEPT

Smart Route Share — putting predictability into ride-sharing

A proposed shared-ride feature that trades some marketplace efficiency for rider control over detours and arrival uncertainty.

Detailed case study (PDF) ↗

Starting problem

Shared rides can become unpredictable when additional pickups create unbounded detours. The concept was motivated by an experience in my family where unpredictable shared-ride stops contributed to missing a train; this motivation is not a substitute for user research. The product hypothesis is that riders value transparent, enforceable detour choices more than a generic promise of savings.

The proposed rider journey

Rider A books a normal ride and opts into sharing, selecting an acceptable detour limit. Only after A's pickup OTP is verified and the trip has started does the system consider a solo Rider B with the same destination and a pickup within A's constraint. Both riders must consent; the MVP permits only one additional rider.

Key product decisions & tradeoffs

  • Detour tolerance as a hard matching rule: favor arrival predictability over maximizing shared occupancy.
  • Match after trip start: prevent premature second-rider coordination before the first pickup is confirmed.
  • Discount only when B actually boards: avoid charging or discounting on an unfulfilled match; A keeps the original fare if B no-shows.
  • One additional rider: simplifies expectations, safety and failure handling for an MVP.

How I'd validate it

Begin with interviews and concept tests on willingness to share under explicit detour limits, then measure opt-in, feasible matches, consent, excess detour minutes, cancellation, complaint rate and contribution margin in a guarded pilot. No live matching engine or outcome results are claimed.
