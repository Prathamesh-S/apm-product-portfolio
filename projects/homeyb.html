PRODUCT LEAD · WORKING ANDROID PRODUCT

HomeyB — building useful local connections without exposing precise location

Led the scope and implementation of a hyperlocal Android product where usefulness, consent, location privacy and safety must work together.

Public evidence repository ↗Prototype walkthrough ↗

The user problem and hypothesis

Someone entering an unfamiliar or changing place may need quick local information or a relevant nearby interaction, but traditional social networks depend on existing contacts or permanent communities. HomeyB explores whether a temporary, approximate-location network can provide useful local responses without exposing exact whereabouts. Repeat usefulness remains to be validated with users.

What I owned

I led problem framing, MVP requirements, interaction rules, prioritization, privacy/safety decisions and technical implementation. A co-founder contributed feature input, physical-device testing, release feedback and Play Console coordination. The resulting React Native / Expo / Firebase Android app includes Nearby, Live Buzz, Interest Hives, Highlights, Activity and mutual connections.

Key product decisions & tradeoffs

  • Approximate H3 location cells: retain local relevance without displaying exact coordinates or movement trails.
  • Foreground presence: favor privacy and battery restraint over always-on discoverability.
  • Mutual consent: allow private conversations only after an accepted connection; include blocking, reports and cooldown rules.
  • Ephemeral discussion versus useful knowledge: Live Buzz expires, while structured Highlights have type-specific lifetimes; dating was excluded because its safety needs differ.

A real investigation: the misleading location error

A tester could publish a Highlight at home but encountered a location-verification error at the office. The initial hypothesis blamed rapid navigation interrupting presence registration. Tracing startup, confirmed presence, backend validation and client error mapping did not establish that as the primary cause. Multiple distinct backend denials were being shown as one location error.

Product response: improve error classification, recovery and observability before adding speculative writes that might mask the underlying issue or increase database cost.

What exists and what comes next

The repository documents a working prototype, two-device controlled walkthrough, product requirements, security rules, analytics instrumentation and validation scripts. Demo profiles and content are test data. The next valuable test is a bounded community pilot measuring first useful interactions, publish reliability, safety and repeat use; statistically meaningful retention or market fit is not established.
